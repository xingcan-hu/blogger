# Product Topic Brainstorm

Produce evidence-backed content opportunities from a product URL. Separate verified facts, observed demand signals, and editorial inference; never invent search volume or social engagement.

## Workflow

### 1. Establish the brief

Infer the audience, publishing language, region, and business goal from the repository and request. Ask only when a missing choice would materially change the research. Record the research date because product claims and trends change.

### 2. Build the product fact sheet

Open and inspect the supplied URL before brainstorming. Review relevant first-party pages such as product, pricing, features, FAQ, blog, privacy, terms, changelog, and documentation.

Capture:

- problem solved and target users;
- verified features, limitations, pricing, free plan, and differentiators;
- common workflows and supported inputs or outputs;
- trust, privacy, legal, or safety constraints;
- claims that require hands-on testing before publication.

Attribute all product claims to the website. If a page is inaccessible, say so and do not fill the gap from memory.

### 3. Research search demand

Search the web in the target language and market. Use several query shapes:

- category terms: `free PDF translator`, `PDF 翻译`;
- problem terms: `PDF too large to translate`, `扫描 PDF 怎么翻译`;
- job-to-be-done terms: `translate research paper PDF`;
- audience terms: `PDF translator for students`;
- comparison and alternative terms;
- question patterns: what, how, why, safe, free, best, versus;
- autocomplete, related-search, People Also Ask, forum, and video-result wording when visible.

Treat repeated query language and recurring result types as demand signals. Do not report exact volume, CPC, difficulty, or trend direction unless a source actually provides it. Label unavailable metrics as unavailable.

### 4. Research current social attention

Search recent public discussions across platforms relevant to the target market. For Chinese-language audiences, consider 知乎、小红书、哔哩哔哩、微博、微信公开文章 and specialized forums; for international audiences, consider Reddit, YouTube, X, TikTok, LinkedIn, Hacker News, Product Hunt, and specialized forums. Choose platforms that fit the product and audience; do not force every platform into every task.

Look for:

- repeated frustrations and questions;
- surprising use cases, before/after demonstrations, and workflow hacks;
- objections about price, privacy, quality, limits, or trust;
- news hooks, seasonal needs, memes, and terminology used by real users;
- posts or videos with visible dates and engagement.

Record direct links, platform, date, and visible engagement when available. A single viral post is a hook, not proof of durable demand. Distinguish recent momentum from evergreen interest.

### 5. Inspect competitors and content gaps

Review representative ranking pages and competitor content. Identify what is already saturated, outdated, overly generic, unsupported, or missing a useful test. Prefer gaps where the article can add original evidence, a downloadable example, a comparison, or a clear workflow.

Check existing repository posts before proposing ideas. Avoid keyword cannibalization; recommend updating or expanding an existing article when that is stronger than creating a new one.

### 6. Generate and score ideas

Create ideas across four buckets:

- evergreen how-to and problem solving;
- audience or document-specific use cases;
- comparisons, alternatives, and purchase-intent topics;
- timely social or news hooks.

Score each idea using [references/scoring-and-output.md](references/scoring-and-output.md). Scores are editorial prioritization, not traffic forecasts.

### 7. Deliver an actionable brief

Return:

1. a concise product and audience summary;
2. the strongest observed search and social signals with source links;
3. existing-content overlap and gaps;
4. a prioritized topic table with primary keyword, search intent, hook, product connection, evidence, freshness, effort, and score;
5. the top three recommended articles with suggested title and outline;
6. ideas to avoid or merge, with reasons;
7. unknowns and claims requiring hands-on testing.

For this Blogger repository, invoke `blogger-seo` when turning a selected idea into a post. Use `blogger-natural-voice` when drafting or rewriting the article. Use `blogger-images` when preparing screenshots or illustrations.

## Evidence rules

- Prefer current first-party sources for product facts and primary sources for policies or official documents.
- Cite near every time-sensitive claim.
- Label signals as `verified`, `observed`, or `inferred`.
- Never manufacture keyword metrics, platform engagement, customer quotes, testimonials, or trend data.
- Do not treat search-result count as search volume.
- Do not recommend uploading private, confidential, regulated, or personally identifying files to a third-party product without appropriate warnings.
- Mention research limitations when login walls, personalization, geography, or unavailable tools reduce visibility.
